# Brand Concept — guía de preguntas

Las 10 fases de conceptualización del **Brandbook OS generator**, con lo que
pregunta cada una, en qué formato espera la respuesta y qué criterio la da por
buena. Es el mapa del cuestionario, no el resultado: lo que sale al completarlo
es `brand-concept.md`, y la dirección visual vive aparte en `visual-direction.md`.

Las preguntas, etiquetas y ejemplos están tal cual aparecen en el dashboard.

**Cómo leer cada campo**

- **Pregunta** — lo que se muestra bajo la etiqueta del campo.
- **Formato** — cuántas respuestas admite y de qué tipo. Los mínimos son reales:
  un bloque no se da por completo hasta cubrirlos.
- **Filas** — cuando el campo se desglosa, cada línea responde a una etiqueta
  fija. Se exportan emparejadas, de modo que la IA sabe qué corresponde a qué.
- **Ejemplo** — el mismo que trae el dashboard, de una marca de café ficticia.
- **No se pregunta en este bloque** — hojas que el documento final marca como
  derivables: la IA las deduce de lo respondido en lugar de inventarlas.

## 01. Brand Essence

Define the core idea behind the brand and why it matters.

> **Criterio.** If you cannot explain it in one sentence, the brand is still too blurry.

### Core idea

**Pregunta.** What is the brand, what does it do and for whom?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** A premium neighborhood coffee brand that turns everyday rituals into a slower, more intentional way of living.

### Purpose

**Pregunta.** What is the brand trying to improve or change in the world?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** To help people reclaim meaningful moments in a rushed culture.

### Promise

**Pregunta.** What meaningful value does the brand make real for people?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** A calmer, more elevated everyday experience without pretension.

### Value proposition

**Pregunta.** What is the clear benefit the brand provides in the market?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Beautiful design, well-made essentials, and a warm community feeling in one brand.

## 02. Positioning

Clarify the category, target and lines of differentiation.

> **Criterio.** Positioning is not a slogan; it is the precise place the brand owns.

### Category

**Pregunta.** In which category or territory should the brand be recognized?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Premium specialty coffee and lifestyle rituals.

### Audience

**Pregunta.** Who is the brand actually for and why?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Urban professionals and creatives aged 25–45 who care about taste and ritual.

### Market context

**Pregunta.** What is happening in the market that shapes this opportunity?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** A crowded coffee market with strong lifestyle branding and growing demand for more intentional consumption.

### Differentiation

**Pregunta.** What makes the brand meaningfully different from competitors?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Crafted for emotional depth and ritual, not just caffeine intensity.

### Competitive territory

**Pregunta.** What space is the brand trying to own with less friction?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** The intersection between hospitality, design and everyday wellbeing.

## 03. Brand Personality

Capture the human traits and emotional tone of the brand.

> **Criterio.** The tension between two qualities makes the personality memorable.

### Personality traits

**Pregunta.** Choose 3–4 words that describe the brand's character.

**Formato.** 3–4 palabras sueltas

**Ejemplo.** calm · premium · warm · thoughtful

### Desired perception

**Pregunta.** How should people see the brand after interacting with it?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** High-quality, grounded, and emotionally intelligent.

### Tensions / contrasts

**Pregunta.** Which qualities create a memorable contrast? Example: warm + sharp.

**Formato.** Dos términos enfrentados (A + B)

**Ejemplo.** minimal + expressive

**No se pregunta en este bloque.** Emotional territory — se deducen de lo anterior.

## 04. Brand Values

Define the beliefs that guide behavior and decisions.

> **Criterio.** Values are not abstract; they should shape brand behavior, language and choices.

### Core values

**Pregunta.** Name 3 key values that define the brand.

**Formato.** 3 líneas

**Ejemplo.** calm · craft · community

### What each value means (and does not)

**Pregunta.** One line per value: what it means, then what it never means.

**Formato.** 3 líneas etiquetadas

**Filas.** Una por cada valor definido arriba; cada fila toma ese valor como etiqueta.

**Ejemplo.** Intentional, never empty · Human, never industrial · Welcoming, never curated

---

## 05. Conceptual Territory

Define the conceptual world behind the brand.

> **Criterio.** The concept should feel like a world people can enter, not just a moodboard.

### Central concept

**Pregunta.** What is the main idea or narrative the brand is built around?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** The morning ritual as a private ceremony.

### Key ideas

**Pregunta.** List the 3 concepts that support the central idea.

**Formato.** 3 líneas

**Ejemplo.** slow rituals · material care · quiet confidence

### Metaphors

**Pregunta.** Name 1–2 metaphors or images that naturally describe the brand.

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** A well-made ritual, like a gentle reset for the day.

### Cultural references

**Pregunta.** Add 2 references that help define the world of the brand.

**Formato.** 2 referencias (texto, imagen o PDF)

**Ejemplo.** Scandinavian hospitality design · independent coffee culture · minimalist editorial photography

**No se pregunta en este bloque.** Associations — se deducen de lo anterior.

## 06. Voice & Tone

Set the way the brand speaks and how it adapts across contexts.

> **Criterio.** A strong voice is recognizable even without a logo.

### Voice characteristics

**Pregunta.** Describe the brand's voice in 3 words.

**Formato.** 3 palabras sueltas

**Ejemplo.** warm · precise · understated

### Tone by situation

**Pregunta.** One line per situation: how should the brand sound in each?

**Formato.** 3 líneas etiquetadas

**Filas.** Everyday · High-stakes · Intimate

**Ejemplo.** Warm and close, never familiar · Direct and clear, never alarmed · Quiet, unhurried, almost private

### Expressions to use

**Pregunta.** Name 2 phrases or structures that are aligned with the brand.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** made to be lived with · slow by design

### Expressions to avoid

**Pregunta.** Name 2 words or expressions the brand should avoid.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** ultra · hype

**No se pregunta en este bloque.** Vocabulary — se deducen de lo anterior.

---

## 07. Messaging

Translate the brand into clear messages and narrative pillars.

> **Criterio.** Messaging should connect strategy with emotional clarity.

### Core message

**Pregunta.** What is the single message the brand should consistently communicate?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Slow down, drink better, and make the everyday feel intentional.

### Key claims

**Pregunta.** List the 3 strongest claims the brand can own.

**Formato.** 3 líneas

**Ejemplo.** Thoughtful craft · Everyday ritual · Warm premium experience

### Narrative pillars

**Pregunta.** Name the 3 story themes that consistently drive the brand's communication.

**Formato.** 3 líneas

**Ejemplo.** The ritual of the morning · The crafted detail · The feeling of belonging

**No se pregunta en este bloque.** Supporting messages — se deducen de lo anterior.

---

## 08. Distinctive Codes

Identify the recurring ideas and behaviors that make the brand recognizable.

> **Criterio.** Distinctive codes are the details that make the brand instantly recognizable.

### Conceptual codes

**Pregunta.** What recurring concepts should always appear in the brand's language or visuals?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Warm neutrals, natural materials, quiet contrast, everyday rituals.

### Recurring ideas

**Pregunta.** Name the 2 ideas that give the brand a recognizable rhythm.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** ritual · quiet confidence

**No se pregunta en este bloque.** Recognizable brand behaviors — se deducen de lo anterior.

---

## 09. Boundaries

Define what the brand should never say or imply.

> **Criterio.** Boundaries protect the brand from dilution and strategic drift.

### What the brand should never communicate

**Pregunta.** What should the brand never claim, promise or imply?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Never promise status, never market speed as virtue, never use generic hype language.

### Concepts to avoid

**Pregunta.** Name 2 ideas or themes that do not belong to the brand.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** trend-chasing · over-polished luxury

### Perceptions to avoid

**Pregunta.** What negative or unintended perception should the brand prevent?

**Formato.** Texto libre

**Ejemplo.** Not cold, not performative, not generic or disposable.

---

## 10. Creative Principles

Translate the brand into creative decisions for campaigns, content and storytelling.

> **Criterio.** Principles make it easier to decide what fits and what does not.

### Principles for campaigns

**Pregunta.** Name 2 rules every campaign must respect.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** Grounded before impressive · One idea per campaign

### Principles for content

**Pregunta.** Name 2 rules that keep content consistent across formats.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** Useful before clever · Keep a calm visual rhythm

### Principles for storytelling

**Pregunta.** Name 2 rules for narratives that feel on-brand.

**Formato.** 2 líneas

**Ejemplo.** Lead with the ritual, not the product · End on a feeling, not a claim
